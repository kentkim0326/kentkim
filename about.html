About

Kent Kim 김형섭

He paints in black and white, with silver and gold acrylic. Hangul, Chinese characters and the Roman alphabet are written into one another until the line no longer belongs to any single script.

He was sixteen when his family left Seoul for New Jersey in 1989. He began painting with Hangul almost at once. The three scripts in the work are not a design decision; they are the condition of having grown up between two places, set down in one gesture.

In 1990 and again in 1991 he was selected to represent the State of New Jersey, and his work was shown at the Morris Museum in Morristown. He exhibited in New York in 1992.

He studied economics at Harvard University and then spent two decades as an author, broadcaster and composer, writing a daily illustrated column for the Dong-A Ilbo. He returned to painting full time in 2023, held two solo exhibitions in Seoul that year, and began 133 Places.

The gallery

His mother, Kang-Ja Lee, opened the Haenah-Kent Gallery at 568 Broadway in SoHo in 1990, with a parallel space in Gwanhun-dong, Seoul. He was in high school, and built it with her.

Nam June Paik was one of the artists who came through his mother's gallery. They met when Kent was in his teens.
