Kent Kim
Work 133 Places About

One stroke, three alphabets.

Black and white with silver and gold acrylic. Hangul, Chinese characters and the Roman alphabet written into one another until the line belongs to none of them.

In 1989, at sixteen, Kent Kim moved from Seoul to New Jersey. He began painting with Hangul almost immediately — a script he could still write fluently, in a country where it meant nothing to anyone around him.

That is the question the work has been asking ever since, and it has not changed: what happens to a language when it arrives somewhere it cannot be read. Since 2023 he has been asking it on site, in one hundred and thirty-three places across Korea.
