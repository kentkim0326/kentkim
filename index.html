I paint in public.

Black, white, silver and gold. Hangul, Chinese characters and the Roman alphabet enter the same line and lose their borders.

A letter becomes a limb, a character becomes a doorway, a word becomes architecture. Kent Kim grew up between Seoul and New Jersey and has spent his working life moving between the two languages. The paintings are what that movement looks like when it is set down in one gesture.

He works fast, in one sitting, in front of whoever is watching. Between September 2023 and 2024 he carried that practice the length of South Korea and made one hundred and thirty-three paintings on location — each one taking its subject from where it stood, each one filmed from the first mark to the signature.
